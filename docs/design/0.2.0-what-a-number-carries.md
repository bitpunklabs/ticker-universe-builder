# 0.2.0 — What a number carries

**Status:** implemented in 0.2.0. The contract changed before the code did
([CONTRIBUTING](../../CONTRIBUTING.md)), and this is that change written down first. Two things
came back different from the design and are recorded at the bottom rather than edited into the
text above them — a design note that quietly matches what shipped is not a record of anything.

Nine findings came out of an audit of the selection logic. Two of them turned out to be already
implemented. The remaining seven collapse into **four changes**, because most of them are the
same mistake wearing different clothes.

## The mistake they share

This skill's position, stated in `tier-profiles.md` and proven by removing the theme cap, is
**disclose, do not constrain**. A theme is not capped; it is weighted, and where a universe
concentrated is reported. A bucket is not enforced; its drift is reported.

Every finding below is a place where a quantity is *real, load-bearing and unreported*. That is
the same failure as a cap, arrived at from the other side: a fact nobody can see is a judgement
nobody can check. The fix is never a new constraint. It is a number in the report.

Nothing here adds a concept. Three of the four changes make an existing number honest; the
fourth adds one line to a report that already exists.

---

## 1. Every number carries its denominator

*Findings: scoring rewards missing metrics; a percentile's population is undeclared.*

### The defect

`metric_score` averages the weighted fields that are **present**, dividing by the weight of
those fields only. The denominator shrinks with the data, so absence raises the score:

| Candidate | Score |
|---|---|
| `liquidity 0.95`, three fields absent | **0.95** — wins the seat |
| `0.90 / 0.85 / 0.80 / 0.75`, all four present | **0.85** |

This is live: 5% of eligible candidates in the shipped examples carry no `independence`.

What makes it more than arithmetic is where the absence comes from. `measurement.md` requires
that a ticker with no usable volume "gets no liquidity score rather than a guessed one". The
honesty rule manufactures precisely the input the scoring rule rewards. Two rules, each correct
alone, pulling against each other.

The second half is the same shape one level down. `liquidity` is a cross-sectional percentile
over the price table handed in, and a seed holds sixty to a hundred and twenty names. A candidate
at 0.9 against a hundred researched names may be 0.4 against its market. The number is honest
about its *method* — window, source and formula are all recorded — and silent about its
*population*, so two universes of one market are not comparable even though both look precise.

### The change

**Score against the full denominator.** `metric_score` divides by the total weight of the
bucket's fields, present or not. Absence then costs exactly what the missing field weighs,
which is the only answer consistent with a system that refuses guessed statistics: a metric you
did not measure does not earn a seat, and it does not get out of the way either.

**Report what a number was computed over.** A member records `scored_on` — how many of its
bucket's weighted fields had values. `measurement` records `population`: the count of distinct
tickers the cross-sectional percentiles were taken against.

### Why not the alternative

Treating an absent field as its bucket's floor also removes the bug, but it invents a per-bucket
floor constant that nothing else in the system needs, and it silently substitutes a number the
research did not produce. Full denominator introduces nothing and says the true thing.

### Cost

One divisor. Two additive fields. Membership will move — that is the point — and the examples
will move with it, which is the diff to read rather than the diff to fear.

## 2. Three silent quantities become reported ones

*Findings: the breadth floor's cost; the bucket remainder; drift measured against the wrong
population.*

### 2a. The floor's cost, at design time

The breadth floor gives every reachable theme one seat before weight is consulted. It takes
**38–66% of the Light budget, median 56%**; Brazil spends 23 of 35 seats this way. Which means
that in a small market, tuning `weight` tunes only the remaining third.

`taxonomy --check` errors when the reachable themes *exceed* the target and says nothing below
that, so all fourteen markets live in its silence.

**Change:** warn when the floor takes more than half the target, naming both numbers. The floor
is not the problem — it is what makes this a survey rather than a shortlist. The author not
knowing the price is the problem, and the warning belongs where they are already looking.

### 2b. The remainder stops favouring core

Bucket quotas are `math.floor(target × share)`. Nine of the fourteen markets lose a seat to
rounding, and the second selection pass hands it to `core` every time, because core is 80% of
the bench and sorts first. Brazil's tactical bucket is entitled to `0.05 × 35 = 1.75` and gets 1.

**Change:** allocate bucket quotas by largest remainder. Brazil's tactical bucket gets 2.

This is a consistency fix more than a behaviour fix. The skill already apportions *themes* by
Sainte-Laguë and then apportions *buckets* by flooring and spilling. One discipline, used twice.

### 2c. Drift compares like with like

Theme drift fires when `held >= 5 and held > 2.5 × expected`, where `expected` comes from
apportionment weight. But `required` benchmarks and anchors never go through apportionment.
Korea's `00_A` holds 3 against an expectation of 1, and two of the three are required benchmarks.
Every market carries two or three required seats and they all sit in benchmark themes, so the
distortion is universal. `THEME_DRIFT_FLOOR = 5` is the only reason it has never surfaced.

That floor is itself hiding the rest. At Light most themes expect one to five members, and the
largest theme in any shipped example holds seven, so a theme at four times its share is
currently invisible in the tier most people use.

**Change, and the order matters:** subtract required seats from both sides of the comparison
first, then lower the floor. Doing it the other way round makes Korea false-positive on day one.

### Cost

One comparison, one allocator, one subtraction. No new fields, no new concepts, and 2c leaves
the rule simpler than it found it — apportioned seats judged against apportioned expectation.

## 3. Shake the ruler

*Finding: nothing measures stability, which is the property the instrument is sold on.*

`--seed` preserves incumbents across an explicit rebuild and `maintain` caps turnover per review.
Neither answers the question index construction asks first: **if a measured number moves by one
percent, how much of the membership changes?** A pool whose stated purpose is low turnover has no
measurement of its own churn under perturbation.

### The change

`build` reports one number. Re-run the selection with every measured metric perturbed by ±1%,
deterministically, and report the share of members common to all three runs.

```
Stability: 0.96 — 77 of 80 members survive a ±1% perturbation
```

### Why a number and not a buffer

The industry answer is a buffer: a candidate clears a higher bar to enter than to stay, so noise
at the boundary cannot flip a seat. It works, and it may well be right here later.

It is the wrong thing to build first, for the reason this skill already knows. A buffer is a
constraint, and this design has spent two releases replacing constraints with disclosures because
a constraint hides the quantity it acts on. Measure the churn, publish it, and let a real number
argue for a buffer. If stability comes back at 0.98 there was never anything to fix; if it comes
back at 0.7, the buffer has evidence behind it instead of an analogy.

### Cost

Two extra selection passes over an in-memory candidate list, and one line in the report. No new
input, no new policy constant, no state.

## 4. `evaluate` gains the pair it cannot currently see

*Finding: nothing checks whether two members are the same exposure.*

`factor_r2` measures a member against the factor complex or its theme. The `independence` section
of `evaluate` checks declared independence against an OLS on the benchmark basket. **No statistic
compares two members to each other.** The breadth floor guarantees that every theme is
represented; nothing guarantees that two observation posts were not built on the same hill.

For an instrument whose scarcest resource is a seat — and the floor already spends 56% of them —
a redundant pair is the most expensive error available, and the one a reader is least able to
catch by eye.

**Change:** a `redundancy` section in `evaluate` reporting the most correlated member pairs over
the window. Disclosure, not a gate: two names can be highly correlated and both belong, and the
reader is better placed to judge that than a threshold is.

`evaluate` already takes the price table this needs. No new input.

## Two findings that were already shipped

Recorded because an audit that quietly drops its own false positives is not an audit.

- **The theme weights have a calibration path.** `evaluate`'s `themes` section already reports
  realised volatility per theme and its share of the total, and `evaluation.md` already names
  the theme weights as what it recalibrates. Nothing to build.
- **Coverage of what was never proposed.** `evaluate`'s `coverage` section already reports how
  many of the largest moves in the pool the universe held and names the ones it missed, with
  `member_share_of_pool` beside it so a narrow price table cannot fake a good score.

Both are unexercised for the same reason: no real window has been run through them. That is a
data gap, honestly declared in the README's Known limits, and not a design gap. It is also the
gap that gates every constant in this skill from ever moving.

## What came back different

Two of the four changes did not survive contact unchanged. Both corrections point the same way,
and it is the direction this whole note is about: a number that is real is worth disclosing, and
a *threshold* is worth almost nothing until something has been measured against it.

**2a's warning threshold was wrong, and its own measurement said so.** The design asked for a
warning when the breadth floor takes more than half the target. The floor takes 38–66% of Light
across the fourteen reviewed tables, so that threshold fires on ten of fourteen *correct* inputs
— and the test that pins every shipped table to a clean `taxonomy --check` failed, which is the
check doing its job. A warning that fires on the majority of good inputs is not a finding, it is
the fastest way to teach an author to stop reading warnings. So the number is disclosed on every
run as `floor_share`, and the warning moved to 75%, where weight really has almost nothing left
to order. Disclose the quantity; warn only where the quantity is alarming.

**3's perturbation had to be drawn per ticker, not applied uniformly.** "Perturb every measured
metric by ±1%" is a rescaling, and `metric_score` is linear, so a uniform shift reorders nothing
and would have reported perfect stability on every input — a number that is always 1.00, which is
worse than no number. Real measurement error is independent per number, so the draw is too, taken
from a digest of `(draw, asset_id, field)` so the reported figure is reproducible on any machine.
Measured at Light across all fourteen markets it lands between **0.90 and 1.00**, median 0.97: uk
loses five of fifty seats to a 1% nudge, kr and fr lose none. That spread is the argument for
having built the measurement before the buffer.

**2b was the change that moved the universes, and change 1 was not.** The audit put finding 1
first because it was the only one selecting a wrong candidate; it is a real fix, pinned by unit
test, and it moved nothing in the fourteen examples because every candidate with a gap was
already losing on the fields it did bring. The seat that actually changed hands was the one lost
to rounding. Nine of the fourteen markets gain an entitlement under largest remainder, and in
four of them the bench had someone to spend it on:

| | out | in |
|---|---|---|
| br | `BBAS3` THEME_LEADER | `AZUL4` LIQUIDITY_SENSOR |
| fr | `VIE` THEME_LEADER | `VK` BETA_SATELLITE |
| jp | `8830` QUALITY_LEADER | `7936` BETA_SATELLITE |
| tw | `1503` QUALITY_LEADER | `2610` BETA_SATELLITE |

Four for four, a core member gives way to the satellite or tactical member the bucket's
fractional entitlement had earned all along — which is exactly what a quota that never reaches
its holder looks like from the outside. tw's seat was settled by the alphabetical tie-break
between two buckets with an identical 0.5 remainder, which is why that rule is tested rather
than incidental.

## Release

0.2.0. Change 1 moves membership, and in a `0.x` line that is a minor bump. Changes 2b and 2c
move it further. The examples move with all of them, in the same commit as the code, and the
diff across fourteen real universes is the review.

Order of work, because two pairs have a required sequence:

1. **1** — the only finding that is selecting the wrong candidate today.
2. **2a + 2b** — independent, small, no shape change.
3. **2c** — required seats out of the comparison, *then* the floor down.
4. **3**, then **4**.
